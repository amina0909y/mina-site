// my story

Where I grew up, how I got here

The short version of a long, winding path — ballet, paper flowers, books, and code, all somehow leading to the same place.

I grew up in Tokyo, Japan. Looking back, I don't think I ever expected to end up doing the things I do now — but maybe that's true for most people.

Ballet came first. There was a studio right next to my house, and I started when I was three — more out of proximity than any grand plan. It pulled me in gradually, and by the time I was ten, I was dancing every day. At fourteen, I trained in Berlin for a two-week program; at sixteen, it was the Bolshoi Ballet Academy in Russia. Both were brief, but they made me want more.

At seventeen, in September 2021, I moved to Perm, Russia to attend ballet school full-time — the plan was four years, through to graduation. Then the war started, and I had to leave. It was one of the hardest decisions I've had to make, but I chose to move to London next, to change my environment completely.

Reading came out of that same period. I quit ballet at eighteen, and leaving hit harder than I expected. The first book I picked up was The Upside of Stress by Kelly McGonigal — not exactly light reading, but it was the right book at the right time, and I haven't really stopped since.

Making things came from the same hard stretch. I stayed in London for four months, trying to figure out what came next. Once I was on the other side of it, I started looking for new creative outlets — drawing, coloring, anything that used my hands. Paper flowers turned out to be one of them, and I've been making them since April 2025.

Code came next, mostly by elimination. I started university in London in September 2023. I had no idea what I wanted to do, and I was heartbroken from losing ballet — computer science felt like a skill that could be useful in any field, so it seemed like a safe bet while I figured out the rest.

Now I'm heading into my final year, and if I'm honest, I don't love CS the way I thought I might. I've been thinking about ways back into the ballet world that aren't performing — maybe the business side, maybe something creative like marketing. I don't have it fully figured out yet, and I'm trying to be okay with that for now.
